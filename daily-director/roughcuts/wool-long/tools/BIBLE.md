# THE RESCUER SYNDROME - long wool version - character bible

Locked 10.9.2026. Every frame in this film references one of these five masters.
Paste the strings verbatim. Do not paraphrase them into a new prompt.

## The photographic grammar, shared by every frame

1970s Kodachrome documentary photograph, faded dyes, a slight cyan shift in the
shadows and a warm cast in the highlights, medium format, shallow depth of
field, soft daylight from the left, the real interior behind gone to blur.
Grainy and slightly soft, the colour of a family slide left in a drawer for
fifty years. Photographed straight on at eye level.

## The puppet grammar, shared by the three roles

A life-size theatrical puppet head, not a person. The face is fabric-covered
carved wood painted matte, the weave of the canvas showing faintly through the
paint, a fine web of age cracks across the cheeks, a seam running down behind
each ear. The expression is carved and fixed and does not change. The eyes are
large and glossy with heavy painted lashes that radiate like stitched thread,
the brows painted on in one confident stroke, the lips a single flat colour.
The whole skull is covered by a dense helmet of hand-made wool pompoms packed
tight, far too big for the face. No strings, no rods, no visible rig: these
puppets stand on their own.

## The colour lock. This never changes, in any shot, for the whole film.

| role | wool | garment | painted face |
|---|---|---|---|
| THE RESCUER | bone white and cream pompoms | off-white shaggy wool collar | pale lids, pale rose lips |
| THE VICTIM | dove grey and slate pompoms | grey felted wool collar | grey lashes, greyed mauve lips |
| THE PERSECUTOR | charcoal and near-black pompoms | black shaggy fur collar | hard black brows, dark oxblood lips |

## The two humans

The only flesh in the film. Everything else is an object.

- THE MAN: a real human face, forty, tired, theatrical stage makeup on real
  skin, a rust-orange wool pompom headpiece. He is the one the roles are put
  onto. He carries the existing `bare` master from the short wool version.
- RAFIK, THE NARRATOR: a real human face, sixty, grey close beard, wire
  glasses, calm, theatrical stage makeup on real skin, a muted terracotta wool
  pompom headpiece, seated in a worn armchair, speaking straight to the lens.
  Clearly older and clearly different from THE MAN so the two are never
  confused.

## Why the roles are puppets and the people are not

The film says a role is something put onto a person. Making the three roles
literal objects, and leaving only two faces made of skin, says that before the
narration does.
